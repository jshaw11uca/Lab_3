The kitchen was still holding onto the cool hush of early morning when the butter hit the cast-iron skillet. It hissed softly, a warm, rich aroma rising to meet the gray light filtering through the blinds. Outside, the world was already starting to wake up, but inside, time slowed to the gentle sizzle of frying eggs.

I cracked two of them into the center of the pan. The whites spread, turning opaque against the dark metal, while the yolks settled like glossy, golden suns. Next to the skillet, the toaster dinged, releasing two slices of sourdough, pale yellow steam swirling into the air.

There is an art to the morning ritual, a quiet solitude before the rush of the day demands attention. I poured black coffee into a chipped stoneware mug, watching the steam curl against the windowpane.

I sat at the small wooden table in the corner, scraping a slab of salted butter across the rough surface of the toast. With the edge of my fork, I punctured one of the yolks. It broke smoothly, spilling across the plate, rich and velvet. The first bite brought the crisp crunch of char, the creaminess of the egg, and the sharp, bright saltiness of the butter—a simple, perfect harmony.

I took a slow sip of hot coffee, feeling its warmth spread all the way down. The clock on the wall kept up its steady tick, but for these few minutes, surrounded by the smell of toasted grain and fresh coffee, the day belonged entirely to me.
