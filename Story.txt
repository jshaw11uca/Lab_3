The kitchen was still holding onto the cool hush of early morning when the butter hit the cast-iron skillet. It hissed softly, a warm, rich aroma rising to meet the gray light filtering through the blinds. Outside, the world was already starting to wake up, but inside, time slowed to the gentle sizzle of frying eggs.

I cracked two of them into the center of the pan. The whites spread, turning opaque against the dark metal, while the yolks settled like glossy, golden suns. Next to the skillet, the toaster dinged, releasing two slices of sourdough, pale yellow steam swirling into the air.

Then the quiet split wide open with a low, bone-deep rumble.

The floorboards groaned under my feet, trembling violently enough to rattle the stoneware mugs in the cabinet. Before I could reach for the stove handle, a sharp crack echoed through the room. A jagged fissure tore right down the center of the linoleum, splitting the kitchen floor in two. Orange heat burst through the gap, instantly scorching the baseboards and drowning out the smell of browning butter with the suffocating stench of sulfur and raw earth while molten lava began to bubble.

I stumbled back against the counter as the floorboards buckled, folding downward into a churning, incandescent river of molten rock that bubbled up from below. Glowing specks of ash danced in the air, catching the blinds and igniting them in tiny bursts of flame.

Right in the middle of this roaring hellscape sat the stove, perfectly balanced on a shrinking island of subfloor. Miraculously, the cast-iron skillet hadn't tipped over.

Through the thick, shimmering heat haze, I watched as the edges of the sourdough began to char rapidly. The pan was getting hotter by the second, fueled by the hellfire surging just inches beneath it. The egg whites whipped into a wild, frothy sizzle, the yolks trembling like liquid gold against the blinding glow of the lava.

Grabbing a silicone oven mitt, I lunged across the gap, snagging the pan's handle just as its wooden support beam gave way to the magma below. I pulled it back to safety on my remaining patch of countertop, grabbed my mug, and leaned against the sink.

I took a slow sip of hot coffee, feeling its heat match the roaring inferno at my feet. The morning quiet was shattered, but as I ate my perfectly flash-fried eggs over an active volcanic fissure, the day still belonged entirely to me.
